Introduction
In today’s era, digital technology has changed the way of conducting organisational work based
on the business-driven strategy as an enormous number of data is being collected by different
industries. Technological devices such as laptops and smartphones allow organisations and
their stakeholder to create and store a large amount of data of their customers. Furthermore,
these large industries’ data is analysed in different sectors for business decision-making to gain
a competitive advantage in various business areas (Goar & Yadav, 2022). Data analytics is
used by multiple industries for decision-making, such as healthcare, banking, education,
communication, retail, and transportation (Le & Liaw, 2017). In this fourth industrial
revolution, the Internet of Things – IoT (physical device network transfer data from one device
to another without human intervention) enable retailers to improve their decision in customer
experience, supply chain management, new customer segment revenue generation, pricing, and
discounting strategy, and in-store customer behaviour analysis. Moreover, with the help of data
analysis, a retailer, wholesaler, and manager can analyse the market trend and set their
marketing strategy to create a large customer base (Matthew et al., 2015).
This report aims to analyse the dataset and make a data-driven decision in the retail superstore
supplying office equipment product. Moreover, the dataset is selected from Kaggle, and various
scholarly journals and articles are selected from the Robertson Library to support the data
analysis results and the arguments. This report will also focus on three major business problems
based on the selected dataset: product analysis, pricing and discounting, and logistic/transport
preference. Retail superstore has an extensive range of product under various brands; thus, it
is worth getting insight into data analytics tools and how it is helpful in the retail sector for
data-driven decision-making. Furthermore, in this report, dataset analysis helps understand
how data analytics allows retailers of office supplies accessories to make decisions in crucial
business areas such as discount, pricing, logistic preference, and product-centric and customercentric business model. Further, this report will discuss the data description of the selected
dataset, data analysis method, and visualisation with the recommended data-driven decision
for the manager.
Data Description
Different industries collect a large volume of data daily, which is difficult to analyse as raw
data. Data collected from various sectors are characterised based on volume (size of data),
variety(different types of data), and velocity(a dynamic form of data) of data which is difficult
sample
to manage because of the complexity of data (Black, 2023). All numerical data are not analysed
similarly because different numbers represent different entities. Major types of data
measurement for the bifurcation of numerical data, which are discussed below (Black, 2023).
1. Nominal: This is the lowest level of data measurement, and it can only be used for
classifying or categorising.
2. Ordinal: This data measurement gives the rank or order for people or objects.
3. Interval: This data measurement measures the distance between numbers when all the
data are in numbers.
4. Ratio: The highest data measurement level represents the distance among the values.

2. Pricing and discounting strategy
Setting appropriate pricing for the target consumer is one of the critical decisions for retail
profitability and customer segmentation. Retailers frequently use pricing discrimination to
boost sales and use price discounts or quantity discounts (Khan & Jain, 2005). In today’s
digitalised world, e-commerce retailers also use advanced technology to deliver the right
pricing offer to the right customer at the right time. Similarly, pricing analysis helps managers
analyse specific patterns or trends in customer buying behaviour, which helps retailers increase
profitability by providing appropriate prices (Bodea & Ferguson, 2014). Moreover, appropriate
discount helps the manager to analyse buying behaviour and customer engagement. In the retail
management context, business issues can arise that what could be the best promotion strategy
to deliver high customer satisfaction and good revenue because discounting and price reduction
are negatively correlated to the firm's income which is presented below in Figure 8.

Figure 8: Predictive Analysis (Co-relation)
As the above correlation shows, sales amount and discounts are negatively correlated as
discount increases and sales order value decreases (-0.08). Additionally, a high discount
negatively impacts profit because a higher discount lowers the profit. Moreover, a reduced
pricing strategy also increases the order quantity from regular sales to the amount of product.
Thus, appropriate discounts and pricing will only sometimes cause loss or reduce profit.
Similarly, as demonstrated in predictive analysis, shipping cost is highly correlated with sales
order value (0.767), which means increasing order value will lead to higher shipping costs.
However, a high correlation between shipping cost and sales order value can only be predicted
for some products and sales orders as many other variables affect shipping cost per order, such
as shipping priority, order priority, delivery days, and types of products. To add to this, it is
noted that there is no significant correlation between delivery days and profit in retail / Ecommerce superstore, which demonstrate that customer probably would not care about delivery
time. However, in this fourth industrial revolution, it is the prime responsibility of retailers to
ensure that product delivery is timely because it may cause additional costs to the company.
Furthermore, the correlation of quantity in the order and sales value is positively related as
quantity increases, sales value increases, but this is not applicable in all the office supplies
retailing as many products lines high-value costs such as tables, copier machines, phones,
binders, chair, and storage.

Moreover, as presented in Figure 9 above, diagnostic analysis (ANOVA) suggests a significant
difference in discounts available for the product among various customer segments,
significantly affecting the profit of the superstore. Predictive analysis by regression model and
diagnostic analysis done by Danziger et al., (2014) demonstrate that consumers are more likely
to buy products from retailers who offer discounted prices every day or continuously.
Additionally, customers did not prefer the retailer offering infrequent but high discounts and
preferred a retailer offering frequent low discounts. Moreover, price heterogeneity is also
affecting the sales revenue of the product. On the other hand, Koçaş & Bohlmann, (2008)
analyse that some retailers are giving deep discounts and forgo price-sensitive switchers to earn
more profitability within a shorter time and are also incorporating price optimisation benefits.
Managers or retailers should make data-driven decisions to provide lower discounts on
different products under different brands rather than promoting high deals irregularly.
Moreover, the manager needs to focus on the price optimisation strategy so that one lossmaking product can be recovered from the other products and can make trade-offs between
price reduction and revenue. Besides this, competitors’ pricing and discount strategies
significantly affect the existing customer base; thus, the manager can make data-driven
decisions for price optimisation and everyday low pricing.
Logistic/Shipping preference for profitability and customer satisfaction
In today’s era of digital technology, logistic and transportation services is crucial for retailers
and the e-commerce industry. In e-commerce and retail, the most critical aspect is the delivery
mode, delivery time, and services in online shipment to the customer. Retail retailers provide
door-to-door services to increase customer satisfaction in traditional retail business
management. Superstore retailer services differ from e-commerce in terms of delivery
promptness, security, product availability, delivery acknowledgment, and product tracking
(Haron et al., 2017). For higher profitability and customer retention, the manager must identify
the suitable shipping mode and set order priority for better customer service and segmentation.

According to Gümüş et al., (2013), pricing partition for different customers is a common
strategy retailers use. From the descriptive and predictive analysis of different variables such
as price, shipping cost, retail brands, and customer demographics, Gümüş et al., (2013)
analysed two customer segmentation basis in retail. Retailers are likelier to add shipping and
handling costs in the lower order value. Besides this, for heavy and fragile products with very
high shipping costs, retailers charge shipping costs from the customer. Arguably Cachon et al.,
(2018) said that e-commerce retailers use a free shipping threshold policy where customers
order for a predetermined amount of orders. Regression model validation used and predicted
that customers are more likely to order impulse product purchases to increase the threshold
limit for free shipping; however, customers are paying less attention to delivery days when they
have lower sales order amounts (Cachon et al., 2018). Arguably, Youn et al., (2014) said that
profitability and delivery services in retail and e-commerce are steadily declining due to price
competition. He also argues that customers are more likely to be concerned about delivery
accuracy and time in delivery services rather than higher or lower product delivery costs.
Recommendation
Data-driven decision-making plays a crucial role in this digital era, as much data are available
in different industries. For the retail and e-commerce sector, comprehensive data are collected
with the help of other technological devices. The manager uses these data to make data-driven
decisions for competitive advantage, inventory management, pricing strategy, and shipping
preferences for the product and customer segments. It can be recommended for the manager
and retailer that low-performing product analysis is significant to identify the cause of the loss
for superstore table products. Managers and retailers must identify the reason for losing a
sample
specific product. They should focus on strategies to mitigate the loss by analysing market trends
and customer buying patterns. Furthermore, Pricing and discounting strategies are vital in
increasing profitability. It is recommended for the retailer that the superstore should promote
discounting on different products regularly instead of providing deep discounts on infrequent.
Moreover, setting logistic preference shipment mode as per order value and development type
increases customer satisfaction by faster delivery days.
Conclusion
This report is designed to analyse the dataset and make data-driven decisions in contemporary
business issues in office equipment and product supply retail superstores. Throughout the
report, three retail business context data analyses were discussed, such as product analysis,
discounting and pricing strategy, and shipping or logistic preference for the superstore with the
literature review. According to Kumar et al., (2017), market, firm, store, and customer-level
marketing strategies are essential to integrate into the store’s management because only high
discounts and shipping costs are not causes of the loss in the product line. Besides this, frequent
discount on products and price optimisation helps retailers design appropriate pricing strategies
for loyal customers (Danziger et al., 2014; Koçaş & Bohlmann, 2008). On the other hand,
logistic service and order priority are essential factors for customer segmentation based on
order value. Youn et al., (2014) demonstrate that customers are more likely to be concerned
about faster delivery time than delivery cost in retail and e-commerce. Overall, managers in
various industries can make data-driven decisions for better business management based on
different data analysis methods such as descriptive, diagnostic, prescriptive, and predictive
techniques.
