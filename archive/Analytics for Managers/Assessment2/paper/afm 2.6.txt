3.1 Identifying and analysing factors affecting attrition rate in a company

	How much does it cost when an employee decides to leave the company? According to Kennedy (2023), data from Work Institute estimates that each lost employee cost the company, on average, one third of their annual salary. Gallup puts the value even higher, placing the value of a satisfactory replacement employee as equal to one-half to two times the original employee’s salary. This value could be interpreted in various ways, such as the value that the employee brings to the company, cost to train the employee, cost to replace the employee, cost to hire and train the replacement, etc... As a result, if your business is struggling with employee retention, it can significantly affect your bottom line and damage your company's brand in the process. 
	So what makes an employee decides to resign? Quality of work life, professional development, working hours, individual/family reasons, connection with coworkers, welfare, working conditions, compensation, were discovered as the main reasons for resignation (Basariya & Ahmed, 2019). High turnover due to these factors can negatively impact organization image, recruitment cost, company productivity, making employee retention a critical challenge for business (Hassan et al., 2019).
	In this analysis, the variables attrition, overtime, duration at company are used to address the relationship between them.

3.2 Identifying and Analysing Factors that affect Job Satisfaction in a company

You might ask, what does the effect of good job satisfaction affect in a company? According to Ismail & Razak (2016), commitment, employee retention, motivation and productivity, loyalty and confidence, etc.. are improved in companies that have good job satisfaction, and it also lessens negative behaviour such as absentees and turnover. As a result, if your company is struggling with improving job satisfaction, it can affect company performance in the process.
So, what affects job satisfaction in a company? According to Iqbal et al., (2017), organisational culture, benefits, salary, rewards, relationship with supervision, work environment, autonomy, job expectation are some of the factors that affect job satisfaction. Low job satisfaction can impact company performance, making productivity a critical challenge for businesses (Hartika et al., 2023).

According to Seppälä & McNichols, (2022), research shows that leaders who prioritize relationships with their employees and lead from a place of positivity and kindness simply do better, and company culture has a bigger influence on employee well-being than salary and benefits. Seppälä & McNichols also outlined that the five core principles that make all relationships, personal or professional, thrive are transparency and authenticity, inspiration, emotional intelligence, self-care, and values. Thus, lack of relationship satisfaction can result to less productivity and performance, less employee satisfaction, increased stress, and additional costs in the workplace (Bălașa et al., 2023).

As a manager that wants to improve the relationship satisfaction, various programmes such as flexible work times, flexible work place, parental leaves, reducing unnecessary meetings, minimizing administrative tasks, adding work family benefits could improve work life balance (Wood et al., 2020). Thus it could also improve relationship satisfaction in the company.
